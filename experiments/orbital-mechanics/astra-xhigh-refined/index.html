THE MODEL / A QUICK GUIDE

Two burns. One ellipse.

A Hohmann transfer joins two circular, coplanar orbits with an ellipse tangent to both. The first burn changes your orbit. Half a revolution later, the second matches the speed of the destination circle.

01

Going higher?

Accelerate twice. The first burn lifts the far side of the orbit. Gravity slows you on the way up; the second burn speeds you up to circularize.

02

Coming back down?

Decelerate twice. Coast inward, gaining speed. Brake at the low point to stop the far side of the orbit from rising again.

Try it

Choose “To GEO”, then turn off the arrival burn. Seek to arrival and continue: without the second burn, the spacecraft returns along the same ellipse. Re-enable the burn to hold the new circular orbit.

What the numbers mean

  • Δv is the magnitude of the velocity changes, not fuel consumption. Signed burn values show acceleration (+) or braking (−).
  • Radial speed is positive moving away from Earth and negative moving inward. It is zero at each end of the transfer.
  • Ellipticity e is eccentricity: 0 for a circle, approaching 1 for an increasingly elongated ellipse.

Assumptions & limits

A point-mass Earth, a massless spacecraft, circular start and finish orbits, prograde motion in one plane, and instantaneous tangential burns. Kepler's equation propagates the coast analytically. Earth radius is 6,378.137 km; gravitational parameter μ is 398,600.4418 km³/s².

No atmosphere, oblateness, plane changes, finite thrust, other bodies, collision avoidance, rendezvous targeting, or live ephemerides. GEO here means a circular orbit at 35,786 km altitude; this 2D model does not specify inclination or longitude. At large radius ratios, a bi-elliptic transfer may cost less than Hohmann.
