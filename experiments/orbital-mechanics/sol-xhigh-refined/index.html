THE MODEL

Small, precise, and intentionally idealized.

Apsis is a one-burn design workbench for a spacecraft orbiting Earth. Scrub the coast timeline, set a transverse or radial impulse, compare the resulting orbit, then execute it. Use Undo to return to the previous burn point.

What the controls mean

  • Prograde is transverse, perpendicular to the radius, along the orbit's angular-momentum direction. In an eccentric orbit it is not exactly the velocity direction. A negative value is retrograde.
  • Radial points outward from Earth. Negative values point inward. Δv is the vector magnitude of these two components.
  • Burn location is elapsed time along the current orbit, not true anomaly. A full slider spans one period. Escape trajectories show a six-hour coast window.
  • Green is the predicted post-burn orbit. The white-blue path is the current orbit. Escape and collision previews show only the future arc.

Assumptions & limits

Planar, Newtonian two-body motion around a fixed spherical Earth. The spacecraft has negligible mass. Burns are instantaneous; no fuel, mass, engine duration, inclination, drag, Earth oblateness, Moon, Sun, or other perturbations are modeled. Atmospheric reentry and heating are not simulated. A surface intersection stops the coast at 6,371 km. Negative periapsis altitude means the mathematical conic passes inside Earth.

Presets are idealized starting states, not live spacecraft data. Geometry is sampled for display; telemetry and orbital elements come from the analytical state. This tool is for learning and early intuition, not operational flight planning.
