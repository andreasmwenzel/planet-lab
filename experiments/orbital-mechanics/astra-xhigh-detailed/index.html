    FLIGHT NOTES

    A little physics. A lot of possibility.

    Apogee is a planar, Earth-centered orbital mechanics workbench. Plan a two-burn transfer, compare its cost, then fly it. Or try a free burn and see how the whole orbit responds.

    What is calculated

    Newtonian two-body gravity, with Earth fixed at the focus. Position and velocity are advanced using a universal-variable Kepler solver, including elliptical, parabolic, and escape trajectories. The solver uses a bracketed Newton iteration with a relative equation tolerance of 2 × 10⁻¹¹.

    Earth's gravitational parameter is 398,600.4418 km³/s². The spherical surface has a radius of 6,371 km. Burns are instantaneous changes to velocity.

    What is left out

    No atmosphere or drag, oblateness, Earth rotation, third-body gravity, inclination, finite-duration thrust, vehicle mass, or fuel model. A low perigee is flagged, but air is not simulated. A trajectory stops at the spherical surface.

    This is an educational planning tool, not mission-grade flight software. Displayed Δv is a velocity budget, not propellant usage. Orbit lengths and marker sizes are different scales.

    Reading the orbit

    Perigee is the lowest altitude. Apogee is the highest. Eccentricity describes shape: 0 is circular, between 0 and 1 is elliptical, 1 is parabolic, and above 1 is hyperbolic. Open trajectories have no apogee or orbital period.

    The light path is your current orbit. The dashed coral path is a proposed burn. The dotted green ring is the target circular orbit. Path samples show geometry, not equal time steps.

    Making a transfer

    A Hohmann transfer connects two coplanar circular orbits with an ellipse. A departure burn changes the opposite altitude. After half a transfer period, an arrival burn circularizes the orbit. Lowering orbits uses two braking burns.

    The guided planner accepts circular starting states (eccentricity ≤ 0.0001). Time automatically pauses at arrival. Free burns use local radial and transverse components; transverse is perpendicular to the radius and follows your orbit's current direction.
